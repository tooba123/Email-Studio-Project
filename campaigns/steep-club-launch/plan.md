## 1. Campaign Overview

**Campaign name:** The Steep Club Launch — A New Tea Adventure

**Summary:** Re-introduce Kettlebird Tea's monthly tea subscription to the 2,000-person waitlist and turn 300 of them into paying Steep Club members in four weeks through warm, segmented, email-only storytelling.

**Primary objective:** 300 paid Steep Club subscriptions by 3 January 2027.

That is 15% of the 2,000-person waitlist, which is an ambitious target for email alone. The plan treats 300 as the stretch target and tracks progress against weekly checkpoints so the team can adjust early (see Section 7 and Section 9).

**Secondary objectives:**
- Learn which message converts each of the three waitlist groups.
- Build a reusable set of launch emails and a clear subscription page.
- Start the first-party data habit: segment, source, and conversion tracking from day one.

## 2. Target Audience

**Primary audience:** About 2,000 waitlist members who already showed interest in Kettlebird Tea. They are warm, not cold, and are deciding whether The Steep Club is worth a monthly commitment.

**Segment split:** Confirm the real counts before scheduling. The plan assumes the list can be split into three groups; it does not assume equal thirds.

| Segment | Who they are | Motivation | Likely barrier | Message angle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tea-curious beginners | New to loose-leaf tea or still exploring what they like | Want guidance, variety, and a low-risk way to try new teas | Choice overload; fear of wasting money on tea they do not like | "Discover your next favourite tea, one box at a time." |
| Gift buyers | Buying for a tea lover, often for a birthday or thank-you | Want a thoughtful, easy gift that feels personal | Unsure about delivery timing, packaging, and whether the recipient will like it | "A thoughtful tea gift for someone who loves a good cup." |
| Daily tea drinkers | Drink tea most days and want variety without shopping | Want convenience, freshness, and a reliable monthly treat | Worry about commitment, repeats, and value for money | "Your monthly tea treat, without the guesswork." |

**Buying stage:** Consideration to decision. They know the brand and have raised their hand; the campaign needs to remove doubt and make the first step easy.

**Where they spend time:** Email is the only campaign channel. Copy should be mobile-first; many readers will open on a phone.

**Audience notes:**
- Do not use health, detox, weight-loss, or medical claims.
- Do not use pressure language such as "Hurry!" or "Buy now!"
- Keep delivery, payment, and cancellation details clear and easy to find.

## 3. Key Messages

**Core campaign message:** The Steep Club brings a new tea discovery to your door every month — freshly picked for you.

**Supporting messages:**

| Audience | Message | Proof point to use |
|---|---|---|
| Tea-curious beginners | "Discover your next favourite tea, one box at a time." | Curated selection and tasting notes that make it easy to explore. |
| Gift buyers | "A thoughtful tea gift for someone who loves a good cup." | Gift-ready presentation and a subscription the recipient can enjoy all year. |
| Daily tea drinkers | "Your monthly tea treat, without the guesswork." | A rotating selection that keeps the routine interesting. |
| All groups | "Free shipping on your first box when you join during launch." | Clear offer terms and delivery information. |

**Call to action:** One link per email, to the subscription page. Suggested link text: "Start your subscription" or "Claim your first box."

**Message rules:**
- Warm, curious, approachable. Write like a friendly tea guide.
- Use approved phrases such as "Discover your next favourite tea", "Freshly picked for you", "Your monthly tea treat", and "A new tea adventure".
- Avoid "Buy now!", "Hurry, limited offer!", "Best tea in the world", "Luxury tea experience", "Revolutionary", and "Unmissable deal".
- Never claim the tea cures disease, improves health, guarantees weight loss, or is 100% organic without proof.
- Only make claims about quality, ingredients, and origin that can be verified.

**Subject line direction (examples, final copy to follow the email format rules):**
- "Your next tea adventure starts here"
- "Discover your next favourite tea"
- "A monthly tea treat, delivered"

## 4. Channel Strategy

**Channel:** Email only. No paid ads, no social ads, no SMS.

**Why email fits:**
- The audience is a warm waitlist that already asked to hear from Kettlebird Tea.
- Email is low-cost, measurable, and easy to segment by the three groups.
- The campaign can be run with a small budget and a small team.

**Format recommendations:**
- Four core emails per segment, written as 12 variants (4 emails × 3 groups).
- Mobile-first layout, short paragraphs, one clear link.
- Plain-text versions for deliverability and accessibility.
- Plain, friendly subject lines; preview text that adds information rather than repeating the subject.

**Cadence:**
- One core send per week, Monday to Thursday, 9:00–11:00 AM Pakistan time, on the dates shown in the calendar.
- Avoid 24–26 December and 1 January for sends; the campaign stays evergreen but should not compete with peak holiday inboxes.
- Week 4 includes a final reminder only if the campaign is behind the weekly checkpoint.

**Effort level:** Medium. Copy, segmentation, and QA take most of the time; distribution is low effort.

**Budget note:** No media spend. The small budget covers the email platform, copy and design production, and the free first-box shipping subsidy.

**List and deliverability:**
- Send only to opted-in waitlist members.
- Suppress unsubscribed, bounced, and complained addresses.
- Authenticate the sending domain (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) before launch.
- Test every variant with internal addresses and check links, rendering, and footer.
- Keep the unsubscribe link clear and working.

## 7. Success Metrics

**Primary KPI:** 300 paid subscriptions by 3 January 2027.

**Secondary KPIs:**

| Metric | Target | How it will be tracked |
|---|---|---|
| Overall waitlist conversion | 15% (300 of 2,000) | Subscription page sign-ups divided by delivered emails |
| Tea-curious conversion | 10–12% | Segment-tagged links and checkout |
| Gift-buyer conversion | 18–20% | Segment-tagged links and checkout |
| Daily-drinker conversion | 15–18% | Segment-tagged links and checkout |
| Deliverability | 98% or higher | Email platform report |
| Open rate | 45% or higher | Email platform report |
| Click-through rate | 10% or higher | Email platform report |
| Subscription page conversion | 25% or higher | Page analytics and checkout |
| Unsubscribe rate | Below 1% per send | Email platform report |
| Support questions | Fewer than 50 during the campaign | Shared inbox log |

**Weekly checkpoints:**

| Week | Target subscriptions | Cumulative target |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 (7–13 Dec) | 90 | 90 |
| Week 2 (14–20 Dec) | 90 | 180 |
| Week 3 (21–27 Dec) | 60 | 240 |
| Week 4 (28 Dec – 3 Jan) | 60 | 300 |

**Funnel reality check:** Hitting 300 means 15% of the whole list subscribes. A typical warm-email funnel is lower than that. Treat 300 as the stretch target, watch the Week 1 checkpoint closely, and use the optional Week 4 reminder if the campaign is behind. If the campaign is far below trajectory, the team should decide whether to extend the window, add a lower-friction offer, or revise the target rather than adding pressure tactics.

**Reporting cadence:**
- Daily check-in during Week 1.
- Weekly summary every Monday during the campaign.
- Final report by 5 January 2027.

## 8. Budget Allocation

The budget is small and email-only, so there is no paid media line. The main variable cost is the free first-box shipping subsidy.

| Category | Share of budget | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Email platform and tools | 15% | Sending, segmentation, reporting |
| Copy and design production | 25% | 12 email variants, plain-text versions, page review |
| Free first-box shipping subsidy | 50% | 300 redemptions × [shipping cost] — confirm unit cost |
| Contingency | 10% | Rework, extra test sends, unexpected production |
| Paid media | 0% | Out of scope |

**Unit economics check:** At 300 subscriptions, the shipping subsidy is 300 × the per-box shipping cost. Confirm that number before launch. If the total subsidy is too high for the budget, adjust the offer terms or the price rather than reducing the quality of the emails.

**Production vs distribution:** Production is the larger share (copy, design, QA). Distribution is the email platform. There is no media spend.

## 9. Risks and Mitigations

| Risk | Likelihood / impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| 300 subscriptions is too ambitious for email-only in four weeks | High / High | Segment tightly, send four focused emails, make the offer clear, track weekly checkpoints, and decide early whether to extend the campaign or revise the target. |
| December is a busy inbox month | Medium / Medium | Keep the campaign evergreen, send Tuesday to Thursday, avoid 24–26 December and 1 January, and keep emails short and mobile-first. |
| Deliverability or consent problems | Medium / High | Send only to opted-in addresses, authenticate the domain, suppress bounces and unsubscribes, use plain-text versions, and test every send. |
| Payment or delivery friction in Pakistan | Medium / High | Confirm payment methods, courier coverage, delivery times, and shipping costs before launch. Put the key details in the FAQ and on the subscription page. |
| Brand voice or claim slip | Low / High | Run every email through the brand rules: no health claims, no pressure language, no exaggerated claims. Use only approved phrases and verifiable proof points. |
| Gift-buyer message feels out of season | Medium / Medium | Frame gifting as year-round: birthdays, thank-yous, and "just because", not holidays. |
